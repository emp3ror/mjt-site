**Your profile:** comfortable programmer · goal = understand & build from scratch · math currently rusty

**Target definition of "hero":** you can build a GPT-style language model from scratch, explain every line, fine-tune existing open models, and reason about the field like a practitioner — not just call an API.

**Realistic timeline:** ~6–9 months at 8–10 focused hours/week. Faster if you binge, slower if life happens. It's not a race.

## How to use this roadmap

Three rules that matter more than the resource list:

1. **Just-in-time math, not math-first.** Do *not* spend two months on linear algebra before touching a neural net. Learn the specific math when the code demands it. Your rusty math is a feature here — you'll relearn it grounded in something concrete, which sticks.
2. **Build before you read the theory in full.** For every phase there's a "build this" item. The reading exists to explain the thing you already made work.
3. **The spine of this whole plan is Andrej Karpathy's "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" series** (free, YouTube). It's almost literally designed for your exact request. Everything else supports it.

## Phase 0 — Tooling setup *(≈1 week, fast for you)*

You already code, so this is just aligning to the ML stack.

- **Python** — if you're not already fluent, spend a few days; the syntax is trivial for you, focus on idioms.
- **NumPy** — array/vector thinking. This *is* the mental model for everything later.
- **PyTorch** — the framework you'll use to build from scratch. Just install it and run the official "60-minute blitz" tutorial to get oriented.
- **Environment** — Jupyter notebooks locally, plus **Google Colab** (free GPU) for anything that needs one.

**Resources:**

- [Install PyTorch](https://pytorch.org/get-started/locally/)
- [PyTorch: Deep Learning with PyTorch — A 60 Minute Blitz](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/beginner/deep_learning_60min_blitz.html)
- [NumPy quickstart](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/user/quickstart.html)
- [Google Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/)
- [Project Jupyter](https://jupyter.org/)

**Build this:** a notebook that loads a CSV, does some NumPy array math, and plots something with matplotlib. Trivial — the point is a working environment.

## Phase 1 — Just-enough math *(≈2–3 weeks, ongoing thereafter)*

Intuition-first, visual, no proofs. You are building *fluency*, not rigor.

- **Linear algebra** → 3Blue1Brown, *Essence of Linear Algebra* (YouTube). Vectors, matrices, matrix multiplication, dot products. This is 80% of what you need.
- **Calculus** → 3Blue1Brown, *Essence of Calculus*. You only need: what a derivative is, the chain rule, and what a gradient means. That's it for now.
- **Probability** → distributions, sampling, expectation, and what "softmax turns numbers into probabilities" means. Light touch.

**Resources:**

- [3Blue1Brown — Essence of Linear Algebra (playlist)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZHQObOWTQDPD3MizzM2xVFitgF8hE_ab)
- [3Blue1Brown — Essence of Calculus (playlist)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLZHQObOWTQDMsr9K-rj53DwVRMYO3t5Yr)
- [Khan Academy — Probability & statistics](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability) (for the light touch on distributions and expectation)

**Don't finish this phase before moving on.** Get the gist, then come *back* here whenever a later phase uses something you forgot. Watch the relevant 10-min video again when you hit it in code.

## Phase 2 — Neural networks from scratch *(≈3–4 weeks)*

The most important phase. Where the black box opens.

- **Karpathy — "The spelled-out intro to neural networks and backpropagation" (micrograd).** You build a tiny automatic-differentiation engine and a neural net *by hand*, from zero. After this, backprop stops being magic.
- **Karpathy — "makemore" (first 2–3 videos).** Build a character-level model; introduces the language-modeling mindset gently.

**Resources:**

- [Video: The spelled-out intro to neural networks and backpropagation: building micrograd](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMj-3S1tku0)
- [Repo: karpathy/micrograd](https://github.com/karpathy/micrograd)
- [Video: The spelled-out intro to language modeling: building makemore](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PaCmpygFfXo)
- [Repo: karpathy/makemore](https://github.com/karpathy/makemore)
- [Playlist: Neural Networks: Zero to Hero](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLAqhIrjkxbuWI23v9cThsA9GvCAUhRvKZ)

**Build this:** follow along and actually type out micrograd yourself (don't copy-paste). Then modify it — add an operation, break it, fix it.

**Checkpoint:** can you explain, out loud, how a gradient flows backward through a two-layer network? If yes, you've cleared the hardest conceptual hurdle in the whole field.

## Phase 3 — Deep learning foundations *(≈4–6 weeks)*

Now generalize from "tiny net by hand" to "real training."

- **Training mechanics:** loss functions, optimizers (SGD → Adam), learning rates, batching, overfitting/regularization, train/val/test splits.
- **Core building blocks:** embeddings, tokenization, activation functions, normalization.
- **Optional breadth:** a light pass on CNNs (images) and RNNs (sequences) so you know what came before transformers and why they were replaced.
- **Reference text:** *Dive into Deep Learning* (d2l.ai, free) — use as a lookup manual, not front-to-back reading. Alternatively, **fast.ai's "Practical Deep Learning for Coders"** if you want a more project-driven parallel track.

**Resources:**

- [Dive into Deep Learning (d2l.ai)](https://d2l.ai/)
- [fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning for Coders](https://course.fast.ai/)
- [PyTorch optimizer docs (SGD, Adam, etc.)](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/optim.html)

**Build this:** train a small network in pure PyTorch on a real dataset (e.g. MNIST or a text classifier), tune it, and get a feel for why training goes wrong.

## Phase 4 — Transformers & LLMs from scratch *(≈4–6 weeks) — the core*

This is where "generative AI" actually lives.

- **Karpathy — "Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out."** You build a working GPT. Self-attention, positional encoding, the transformer block — all by hand. This is the centerpiece of your entire journey.
- **Karpathy — "Let's build the GPT Tokenizer."** BPE tokenization demystified; most people skip this and stay confused forever.
- **Read the original paper, *"Attention Is All You Need"* (2017)** — but only *after* building GPT, so it reads as "oh, I made that" instead of hieroglyphics.
- **nanoGPT** (Karpathy's repo) — a clean, real, trainable implementation to study and tinker with.

**Resources:**

- [Video: Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCc8FmEb1nY)
- [Video: Let's build the GPT Tokenizer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zduSFxRajkE)
- [Repo: karpathy/minbpe](https://github.com/karpathy/minbpe)
- [Repo: karpathy/nanoGPT](https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT)
- [Paper: Attention Is All You Need (arXiv:1706.03762)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)

**Build this:** train your own tiny GPT on a text corpus you care about (song lyrics, code, a book) and watch it generate. This is your "I actually did it" moment.

**Checkpoint:** you can now draw the transformer architecture from memory and explain attention to someone else. That's the hero threshold for understanding.

## Phase 5 — Modern generative AI *(≈4–6 weeks)*

From "I built a toy" to "I know how the real ones work."

- **Fine-tuning & adaptation:** full fine-tuning vs. **LoRA/PEFT** (efficient fine-tuning on consumer hardware). Learn to fine-tune an open model (Llama, Mistral, etc.) on your own data.
- **Alignment concepts:** what RLHF / instruction-tuning / DPO do and why base models ≠ chat models. Conceptual, not from-scratch.
- **Practical LLM plumbing:** RAG (retrieval-augmented generation), embeddings/vector search, prompting, and a high-level look at agents — so you understand how apps are actually built on top of models.
- **Diffusion models (optional branch):** if image/video generation interests you, learn how diffusion works (forward noising → learned denoising). Different math flavor, same from-scratch spirit — there are "diffusion from scratch" notebooks that mirror the Karpathy style.

**Resources:**

- [Hugging Face PEFT docs (LoRA and friends)](https://huggingface.co/docs/peft/index)
- [Hugging Face — Fine-tuning a pretrained model](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/training)
- [Paper: Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (InstructGPT / RLHF), arXiv:2203.02155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155)
- [Paper: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), arXiv:2305.18290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290)
- [Paper: Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks, arXiv:2005.11401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)
- [LangChain — RAG tutorial](https://python.langchain.com/docs/tutorials/rag/)
- [Paper: Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models, arXiv:2006.11239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239)
- [Hugging Face — The Annotated Diffusion Model](https://huggingface.co/blog/annotated-diffusion)

**Build this:** fine-tune a small open model on a custom dataset with LoRA, and separately build a minimal RAG pipeline over your own documents.

## Phase 6 — Capstone & specialization *(ongoing)*

"Hero" is proven by shipping something, not finishing a curriculum.

- **Pick one track** to go deep: LLMs/text, image generation, or applied GenAI products.
- **Build one real end-to-end project** you'd show someone — e.g. a fine-tuned model for a specific task, a from-scratch model trained on a novel dataset, or a full RAG/agent app.
- **Stay current:** follow a couple of practitioners, skim key papers (with an LLM to help decode them), reimplement interesting techniques.

**Resources:**

## Anti-patterns that kill momentum

- **Math rabbit-holing** — the #1 killer for your profile. Learn it when code needs it.
- **Tutorial hell** — passively watching without typing the code yourself. Always build.
- **Framework hopping** — pick PyTorch, ignore the debate, move on.
- **Skipping the "boring" fundamentals** (backprop, tokenization) to get to the shiny stuff — those fundamentals *are* the shiny stuff, just disguised.
- **Waiting to feel "ready"** — you learn by building broken things and fixing them.
